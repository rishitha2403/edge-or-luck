Experiment 01 · Overfitting and data snooping

Experiment 03 · The Law of Large Numbers

How many years of data before a Sharpe ratio means anything?

My undergraduate project studies the Law of Large Numbers: when a sample average converges, how fast, and what changes with heavy tails and dependence. A Sharpe ratio is just a sample average divided by a sample standard deviation, so it obeys the same rules. Below are 30 strategies with the same true Sharpe. Watch how far apart their measured Sharpe ratios stay.

Takeaway: the standard error of an annual Sharpe is roughly 1/√years. A 3-year backtest with Sharpe 0.8 cannot be told apart from zero. Heavy tails make the paths jumpier, and real market returns also have volatility clustering, which slows convergence further.

Experiment 04 · Walk it forward

Optimise on the past, then face the future

A long/flat backtest with the guard-rails the earlier experiments call for. Signals use only the closing price up to day t and are traded from day t+1, so there is no look-ahead. Costs are charged on every position change. The lookback is tuned on the training period only, then judged on the test period.

Momentum holds the index while its price is above its moving average. Mean reversion buys when the price falls more than one standard deviation below its moving average (z < −1) and sells once it recovers to the average (z > 0).

It opens on a synthetic demo series. Load a real CSV with Date and Close columns (for example NIFTY 50 history from niftyindices.com or Yahoo Finance, or any NSE stock) to run it on real prices.

Before I believe a backtest

My checklist

Experiment 01How many ideas did I try?Raise the bar with the number of trials. Report the count, not just the winner.
Experiment 02Does it survive realistic costs?Brokerage, STT, exchange fees, slippage and impact. Know the break-even cost.
Experiment 03Is the sample long enough?Sharpe standard error ≈ 1/√years. Fat tails and volatility clustering need even more.
Experiment 04Did it hold out-of-sample?Tune on train, judge on untouched test data, ideally walk-forward across regimes.
Experiment 04Is there a plateau, not a spike?Neighbouring parameters should work too. Sensitivity analysis catches curve fitting.
Built inNo look-aheadSignals at close t, trades at t+1. No future data in indicators, normalisation or universe.
DataNo survivorship biasUse index membership as of each date, including delisted stocks. Today's Nifty 50 list overstates the past.
JudgementIs there a reason it should work?A behavioural or structural story (risk premium, flows, liquidity) makes an edge more likely to persist.
